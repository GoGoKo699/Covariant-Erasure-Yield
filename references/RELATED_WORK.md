# Related work and attribution

The principal addition is a matched, worst-pattern **exact conclusive recovery yield** for isometric U(1)-covariant qubit codes under extensive erasure. The random ensemble, general probabilistic-correction framework, and approximate-decoupling exponent are established ingredients. This comparison was checked against the source versions below on 29 October 2026; it is not an exhaustive priority certificate.

## Probabilistic correction

R. Kukulski, Ł. Pawela, and Z. Puchała, *On the probabilistic quantum error correction*, IEEE Transactions on Information Theory **69**, 4620–4640 (2023), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIT.2023.3254054), [arXiv:2206.05232v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.05232v1).

Theorem 1 gives generalized Knill–Laflamme conditions for conclusive correction. Its proof uses the scalar form of composite Kraus matrices for an accepted identity channel, and the paper develops success-probability optimization. Our [Gram criterion](../science/exact_recovery.md) is a fixed-isometric-encoder reformulation of this framework, with an explicit pseudoinverse filter. It is not a new general correction criterion. The same paper shows that mixed encoders can help in other models, so our all-encoder language is restricted explicitly to covariant isometries.

The quantitative addition requires a common symmetry-constrained encoding and uniform yield over exponentially many erasure subsets. General feasibility of probabilistic correction alone does not supply that statement.

## Random covariant codes

L. Kong and Z.-W. Liu, *Near-optimal covariant quantum error-correcting codes from random unitaries with symmetries*, PRX Quantum **3**, 020314 (2022), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/PRXQuantum.3.020314), [arXiv:2112.01498v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.01498v2), revised 11 April 2022.

This work establishes random covariant encoding constructions and near-optimal approximate erasure correction for U(1) and SU(d). Haar-random vectors within charge sectors, generated by symmetric unitaries, are inherited. Its average- and worst-case purified-distance results do not by themselves imply a nonzero exact accepted probability; the [depolarizing boundary example](../science/exact_recovery.md#why-approximate-correction-is-not-enough) explains why the implication needs another argument.

## Closest extensive-erasure comparison

J. Sheng, *Diffusive Speed Limits for U(1)-Covariant Quantum Error Correction*, [arXiv:2608.04953v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.04953v1), 5 August 2026.

Section II defines complementary Choi-state decoupling distance. The static charge-Haar analysis, including the simultaneous worst-pattern result, already treats adjacent central sectors and extensive erasure. It obtains the inverse-square-root scale and exponentially small corrections to the charge-count benchmark. Those results and the underlying random ensemble are not claimed here as new.

The present task instead requires an accepted linear channel exactly equal to $p\operatorname{id}$. The [all-sector construction](../science/random_construction.md) proves a relative positive-semidefinite lower bound, retaining rare charge sectors and logical coherences, and feeds it into an explicit conclusive decoder. The [offset optimization](../science/asymptotic_optimum.md) supplies the converse over every admissible filling. Changing the decoupling coefficient into a failure coefficient without this accepted-channel argument would not establish the result. No finite-depth or local-scrambler theorem is claimed here.

## Continuous-symmetry obstruction

P. Faist, S. Nezami, V. V. Albert, G. Salton, F. Pastawski, P. Hayden, and J. Preskill, *Continuous Symmetries and Approximate Quantum Error Correction*, Physical Review X **10**, 041018 (2020), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevX.10.041018), [author-hosted published article](https://www.preskill.caltech.edu/pubs/preskill-2020-continuous.pdf), [arXiv:1902.07714](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.07714).

This supplies foundational continuous-covariance constraints and approximate-correction tradeoffs. Heralded failure changes the demanded output guarantee, not the finite-dimensional deterministic obstruction. We retain exact covariance and finite ordinary qubit carriers while allowing a trace-decreasing accepted map.

## Exact erasure-recovery profiles

M. A. Alhejji, N. Lordi, O. Baraskar, A. Shlosberg, and E. Knill, *Constraints on recovering quantum information after erasure*, [arXiv:2607.17319v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17319v2), revised 7 August 2026.

Exact erasure recovery with input-independent success and no-cloning constraints is an established operational framework. The bound on disjoint successful recovery events used in [Interpretation](../science/interpretation.md#the-role-of-one-half) belongs to that framework. Our model fixes a covariant qubit isometry and asks for its optimal extensive-erasure yield, rather than classifying unrestricted recovery-probability bodies.

## Related repository models

[Nonclassical Quantum Access](https://github.com/GoGoKo699/Nonclassical-Quantum-Access/tree/195bc71936d298f01815629d7ca4fc137d022a9e) studies a five-carrier exact-recovery profile beyond all classical mixtures of deterministic access patterns. Its authoritative [model](https://github.com/GoGoKo699/Nonclassical-Quantum-Access/blob/195bc71936d298f01815629d7ca4fc137d022a9e/science/model.md) has unrestricted finite carrier dimensions and no continuous-covariance requirement. Exact conclusive recovery is shared; the finite access-profile separation does not imply the growing-block yield theorem here.

[Quantum Permutation Noise Limits](https://github.com/GoGoKo699/Quantum-Permutation-Noise-Limits/tree/1e329e646b3e91a4d22d9611c6396f28115e9045) studies communication after an unrecorded permutation twirl and local noise. Its [model](https://github.com/GoGoKo699/Quantum-Permutation-Noise-Limits/blob/1e329e646b3e91a4d22d9611c6396f28115e9045/science/model.md) loses positional identity and concerns asymptotic capacities per complete block. Our known-position erasure task has no permutation twirl and transmits one logical qubit. Neither result can be transferred merely because both use symmetry and erasure.

The source comparison supports a distinct exact-yield conclusion without counting shared probabilistic-recovery conditions, charge-Haar ensembles, or charge-distribution identities as independent discoveries. No automatic merger or common-project relationship is assumed.
